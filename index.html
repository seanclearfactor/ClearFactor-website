About us

Built for owners, not around them.

ClearFactor is a Scottish property factor run from Glasgow. We look after the common parts of tenements, modern flatted blocks and new-build developments. The idea is simple: answer people, show our workings, and fix things before they turn into big bills.

Four things we won't compromise on

If we ever fall short on any of these, tell us. We'd rather hear it from you than read it in a review.

We answer

Every message gets a reply from a person within one working day. If something takes longer to sort, we'll tell you when to expect an answer.

We show our workings

Our fees, uplifts and commission are published. Every invoice shows what was done, who did it, and what your share is.

We get ahead of problems

Regular visits to your building, and gutters, roofs and drains looked at on a cycle, not just when water's already coming in.

We're there out of hours

Report a problem at 2am and it's logged at 2am. Real emergencies go straight to an out-of-hours contractor.

What we charge, in black and white.

Management fee

Fixed, per property

Billed quarterly in advance. It covers everything listed under "Included" on our services page. The amount for your building is set out in your development schedule.

Regular contracts

20% on top of the contractor's charge

25% commission

Paid by the insurer and shown on your insurance schedule. Covers placing and renewing the policy and helping owners with claims.

£150 + VAT

The sale pack your solicitor asks for, plus apportioning your final account and refunding your float.

Late payment

£50 admin charge

Only applied after reminders have gone unanswered. Interest may also be added where your title deeds allow it.

Everything here is also in our Written Statement of Services, which every owner receives when we take on their building. If any of these charges change, we'll write to you before it happens. Have a question about a charge? Ask your property manager, and they'll walk you through it.

Switch to us

Changing factor is easier than you'd think.

If your factor doesn't reply, doesn't explain its bills, or hasn't been near your building in months, you don't have to put up with it. Here's how a move to ClearFactor works.

Tell us about your building

What's working, what isn't, and roughly how many flats or houses there are. We'll send you a written quote.

We check your title deeds

Your deeds set out how a factor is changed and how many owners need to agree. We read them and tell you exactly what's needed.

Get your neighbours on board

We'll give you a letter and voting form to share, or write to owners for you. In most buildings, a majority is all it takes.

We handle the handover

Notice goes to your current factor. We collect the records, contracts and insurance details, and every owner gets a welcome pack from us.

Common questions about switching

The things owners usually want to know before they make the move.

How long does it take?

Usually a matter of weeks. Most of the time goes on getting owners to vote and on the notice period your deeds or your current factor's terms require. We'll give you a realistic timeline once we've seen the deeds.

What happens to the float we've already paid?

Your outgoing factor should settle it on your final account, or transfer it if your deeds say so. Any sinking or reserve fund belongs to the building and should follow it. We'll chase this on your behalf as part of the handover.

Will there be a gap in our buildings insurance?

No. A common policy arranged by your old factor often ends when their appointment does, so we arrange new cover to start the day we take over.

Do all the owners need to agree?

Rarely. It depends on what your title deeds say, but in most buildings a majority of owners is enough. Part of our job is working out the exact number for you, so nobody has to wade through legal wording.

We're a new-build development. Can we still switch?

Usually, yes. Developers often appoint the first factor, but owners can normally change it once the rules in the deed of conditions allow. Send us your deeds and we'll tell you where you stand.

Repairs and emergencies

What counts as an emergency?

Water coming into the building, a close door that won't lock, no lighting in the stair, or anything else that puts people or the property at risk. Call our out-of-hours line and we'll get a contractor out.

If it can wait until the next working day, log it in My ClearFactor instead. Out-of-hours call-outs cost more, and that cost is shared by every owner.

Is this a common repair, or mine to sort?

As a rule of thumb, if it's shared under your title deeds (the roof, the close, the rhones, shared pipes, communal gardens), it's common and we'll deal with it. If it only serves your own flat, such as your windows or the pipes inside your home, it's yours.

Not sure? Ask us. We'll check your deeds rather than guess.

How do I know a repair's actually been done?

Every job logged through My ClearFactor shows its progress, from reported to closed. If you think something hasn't been done properly, reply on the job and we'll get the contractor back.

Money and bills

What is the float, and why did I pay it?

The float is a one-off deposit, usually set by your title deeds. It means we can pay contractors promptly without waiting for every owner to settle first. It's held in your building's client account and refunded on your final bill when you sell.

Why is my bill different from last quarter?

Your management fee stays the same, but repairs vary from quarter to quarter. Every line on your invoice shows the work, the contractor and your share, so you can see exactly what's changed.

I'm struggling to pay

Get in touch before the payment is due, not after. We can usually agree a payment plan that works, and we'd much rather do that than send reminders.

What happens if a neighbour doesn't pay?

We follow a set process: a reminder, a final notice, and then, if needed, a Notice of Potential Liability on the property and court action. Unpaid debt affects everyone in the building, so we don't let it drift. If a debt truly can't be recovered, your title deeds may require it to be shared between the other owners, and we'll always explain why before that happens.

Insurance

Why does the building insurance go through you?

Most title deeds for flats require one common policy for the whole building. That way, if there's a fire or a serious leak, there's one insurer and one claim, not a dozen policies with gaps between them.

We receive 25% commission from the insurer for arranging the policy and helping with claims. It's shown on your schedule.

I already insure my flat separately

Let your own insurer know you're covered under a common buildings policy. Many will refund some or all of your buildings premium. Keep your contents cover, though, because the common policy doesn't include your belongings.

How do I make a claim?

Tell us as soon as you find the damage, with photos if you can. For anything affecting the common parts, we register the claim with the insurer, arrange any inspection and keep you updated until it's settled.

Damage to your own contents is a matter for your personal insurer. We can send you the common policy details if they ask for them.

Selling, moving in, and everything else

I'm selling my flat. What do I need to do?

Tell your solicitor we're your factor. They'll ask us for a sale pack, which costs £150 + VAT. Once the sale completes, we'll send your final bill with your float credited back.

I've just moved in. What happens now?

We'll write to you with our Written Statement of Services, your share of the building's costs, and an invite to My ClearFactor. If you haven't heard from us within a few weeks of moving in, give us a shout.

Where can I learn more about owning a flat in Scotland?

Under One Roof is an excellent free guide to shared repairs, title deeds and owners' rights in Scotland. We also publish our own short guides in My ClearFactor.

I'm not happy with something. How do I complain?

Tell your property manager first. Most things can be put right quickly. If they can't sort it, put your complaint in writing and a senior member of the team will look into it and reply in writing.

If you're still unhappy after that, you can apply to the First-tier Tribunal for Scotland (Housing and Property Chamber). Our full complaints procedure is in our Written Statement of Services.

Payment plans

Bigger bill than expected? Talk to us before it's due and we'll work something out.

When do bills go out?

Invoices go out every quarter. Your management fee is billed in advance, and repairs and services are billed after the work has been done. The exact dates and payment terms are in your Written Statement of Services.

What if I pay late?

You'll get a reminder first. If an invoice is still unpaid after that, a £50 admin charge is added, and interest may apply where your title deeds allow it. If you're finding it hard to pay, tell us early and we'll agree a plan instead.
